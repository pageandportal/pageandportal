## Executive summary

- Two strong NZCOM Journal pieces (2025–2026) put **perinatal / maternal mental health** front and centre for LMC midwives: relational continuity helps, but referral pathways and training gaps still frustrate care.
- Health NZ’s **2026 referral guidelines** (consultation with obstetric and related medical services) are live national guidance worth keeping at hand for referral, transfer, emergencies, and declining care.
- RANZCOG has refreshed several Australasian guidelines midwives commonly touch: **Intrapartum Fetal Surveillance** (5th edition, updates through June 2026), **Birth After Caesarean** (Aug 2025), and **Vasa Praevia** (Jul 2025).
- Observational meta-analysis (Kuipers et al., 2025/2026) **reinforces continuity-of-care benefits** beyond RCT evidence — useful when explaining LMC models to colleagues and whānau.
- Cochrane (Lenells et al., 2025): psychosocial **breastfeeding support may help prevent short-term postpartum depression** (low-certainty) and may lengthen breastfeeding — complements, does not replace, mental-health pathways.
- A Spanish multicentre cluster RCT found **midwife-led breastfeeding groups** associated with higher exclusive breastfeeding at 4 months and slightly lower EPDS scores — promising but system-dependent.
- Kaupapa Māori antenatal practice (Te Waiora / Counties Manukau) qualitative work (2026) describes **trust, engagement, and reduced fear of statutory scrutiny** — relevant for culturally safe LMC care.
- Humanitarian midwife-led birthing centres (Rohingya camps) show **respectful midwifery care can work in crisis settings**; transferable lesson is cultural safety and clear referral, not the setting itself.

## NZ / Aotearoa & Australasia

### Caring in action: Midwives’ approaches to supporting perinatal mental health in Aotearoa New Zealand

- **Source / year:** Roia, T., & Barber, C. C. (2025). *New Zealand College of Midwives Journal*, 61, Article 256105.
- **Type:** qualitative research (LMC midwives)
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.12784/nzcomjnl.256105 · PDF: https://www.midwife.org.nz/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/2025-Article-256105-Roia-perinatal-mental-health.pdf
- **Why it matters for LMC practice:** Interviews with NZ midwives interested in mental health highlight screening conversations, “being with,” wraparound connecting, and the need for better upskilling — while naming how hard it is to get secondary mental-health access unless someone is in crisis.
- **Confidence / caveats:** Small, select sample (n=7); strengths-based, not a prevalence or outcome study.

### Women’s experiences of living with maternal mental health conditions and accessing maternal mental health services in Aotearoa New Zealand

- **Source / year:** Taynton, S., Waller, N., & Crowther, S. (2026). *New Zealand College of Midwives Journal*, 62, Article 266201. Published online July 2026.
- **Type:** qualitative research (women’s experiences, Canterbury)
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.12784/nzcomjnl.266201 · PDF: https://www.midwife.org.nz/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026-Article-266201-Taynton-MMH.pdf
- **Why it matters for LMC practice:** Women’s stories underline stigma, self-blame, advocacy needs, and logistics of access; authors note midwives are well placed to screen and educate where there is no formal national MMH screening programme.
- **Confidence / caveats:** Regional qualitative sample (n=7); COVID-era pregnancies; not nationally representative.

### Changing the way we care: restoring Mana to Māori mothers through kaupapa Māori antenatal practice

- **Source / year:** Te Tau, D. (2026). *Frontiers in Global Women’s Health*, 7. Published 3 June 2026.
- **Type:** Kaupapa Māori qualitative / community case study
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.3389/fgwh.2026.1781546
- **Why it matters for LMC practice:** Describes how a Māori-led antenatal model (Te Waiora / HEAL) was experienced as more trusting and engagement-friendly than surveillance-heavy approaches — useful reflection for culturally safe continuity care.
- **Confidence / caveats:** Participant-reported experiences; authors explicitly say this is **not** causal evidence of model effectiveness.

### Guidelines for consultation with obstetric and related medical services (referral guidelines)

- **Source / year:** Health New Zealand | Te Whatu Ora (published 2026)
- **Type:** national clinical guideline
- **Link:** https://www.healthnz.govt.nz/health-professionals/guidance-standards/topic/maternity/national-maternity-clinical-guidelines
- **Why it matters for LMC practice:** Core day-to-day reference for when to consult/refer/transfer, emergencies, transport, and what to do if care is declined — endorsed national guidance for maternity practitioners.
- **Confidence / caveats:** Practice guideline (not a trial). Confirm you are using the current PDF/version on the Health NZ page.

### RANZCOG Intrapartum Fetal Surveillance (C-Obs 1), 5th edition

- **Source / year:** RANZCOG. Fifth edition approved Sept 2025; minor updates March and **June 2026**. Target audience includes midwives in Australia and Aotearoa NZ.
- **Type:** clinical guideline (GRADE)
- **Link:** https://ranzcog.edu.au/wp-content/uploads/Intrapartum-Fetal-Surveillance.pdf
- **Why it matters for LMC practice:** Updated Australasian framework for intermittent auscultation, CTG, and responding to fetal compromise within informed, culturally safe maternity care.
- **Confidence / caveats:** Guideline; local DHB/hospital protocols still apply. Worth verifying any local Te Whatu Ora overlay.

### Related RANZCOG updates (Australasia)

- **Birth After Caesarean (C-Obs 38), v4.1 — August 2025:** https://ranzcog.edu.au/wp-content/uploads/Birth-After-Caesarean.pdf — counselling and care options after previous caesarean (VBAC vs elective repeat).
- **Vasa Praevia (C-Obs 47), v3 — July 2025:** https://ranzcog.edu.au/wp-content/uploads/Vasa-Praevia.pdf — diagnosis/management guidance; timing-of-birth considerations for asymptomatic confirmed cases.

## International highlights

### 1. Midwife continuity of care: systematic review and meta-analysis of case-control and cohort studies

- **Authors / year:** Kuipers, Y., Aitken-Arbuckle, A., Jenkins, H., Watson, S., King, M., & Zemouri, C. (epub 2025 Nov; journal issue 2026 Feb). *International Journal of Nursing Studies*, 174, 105300.
- **Type:** systematic review / meta-analysis (non-randomised studies)
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijnurstu.2025.105300 · PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41330211/
- **Why it matters for LMC practice:** Extends the continuity story beyond RCTs: associations with more spontaneous vaginal birth and lower caesarean, induction, regional analgesia, episiotomy, preterm birth, low Apgar, low birthweight, and neonatal admission — with moderators including complexity, team size, and midwife workload.
- **Confidence / caveats:** Observational designs; residual confounding possible. Complements (does not replace) Cochrane RCT evidence.

### 2. Midwife continuity of care models versus other models of care for childbearing women (Cochrane)

- **Authors / year:** Sandall, J., et al. (2024 Apr 10). *Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews*, CD004667.pub6.
- **Type:** Cochrane systematic review (RCTs)
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004667.pub6 · PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38597126/
- **Why it matters for LMC practice:** Still the cornerstone RCT synthesis for midwife continuity models — fewer caesareans/instrumental births and more spontaneous vaginal births on average; foundational for defending LMC continuity.
- **Confidence / caveats:** Evidence certainty varies by outcome; context and implementation matter.

### 3. Breastfeeding interventions for preventing postpartum depression (Cochrane)

- **Authors / year:** Lenells, M., Uphoff, E., Marshall, D., Wilson, E., Gustafsson, A., Wells, M. B., Andersson, E., & Dennis, C.-L. (2025 Feb 18). *Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews*, CD014833.pub2.
- **Type:** Cochrane systematic review
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD014833.pub2 · Plain language: https://www.cochrane.org/CD014833/DEPRESSN_breastfeeding-interventions-preventing-postpartum-depression · PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39963955/
- **Why it matters for LMC practice:** Low-certainty evidence that psychosocial breastfeeding support **may** prevent some postpartum depression in the short term and may increase longer-term breastfeeding duration — supports integrating feeding support with mental-health awareness, without overclaiming.
- **Confidence / caveats:** Many outcomes very low certainty; few trials; not a substitute for dedicated MMH services.

### 4. Midwife-led breastfeeding group intervention and postpartum depression (multicentre cluster RCT)

- **Authors / year:** Rodríguez-Gallego, I., Vila-Candel, R., Corrales-Gutierrez, I., Gomez-Baya, D., & Leon-Larios, F. (2024 Jan 10). *Nutrients*, 16(2), 227. (Andalusia, Spain; trial period 2021–2023.)
- **Type:** multicentre cluster randomised controlled trial
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.3390/nu16020227 · Free full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10821517/ · PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38257120/
- **Why it matters for LMC practice:** Midwife-led breastfeeding support groups associated with higher exclusive breastfeeding at 4 months (≈70% vs 50%) and a small but statistically significant EPDS difference favouring the intervention — practical model ideas for group postnatal support where systems allow.
- **Confidence / caveats:** Unblinded cluster RCT in one health system; absolute EPDS difference modest; local feasibility in NZ LMC caseloads may differ.

### 5. Water immersion / waterbirth outcomes (large systematic review)

- **Authors / year:** Burns, E., Feeley, C., Hall, P., & Vanderlaan, J. (2022). *BMJ Open*, 12(7), e056517.
- **Type:** systematic review and meta-analysis
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2021-056517 · PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9315919/
- **Why it matters for LMC practice:** For women using water immersion/waterbirth (mostly obstetric-unit datasets), associations include less epidural/opioids/episiotomy and more intact perineum and satisfaction; waterbirth linked to higher odds of cord avulsion though absolute risk remained low — useful for informed choice conversations (including Motueka/Nelson/home water use where applicable).
- **Confidence / caveats:** Mostly observational; setting and care practices vary; cord-avulsion absolute numbers still low but worth knowing.

### 6. Waterbirth and perineal outcomes in planned home birth (Nordic cohort)

- **Authors / year:** Halfdansdottir, B., Ellinger-Kaya, K., Fjosne, K., Lindgren, H., Hegaard, H. K., & Blix, E. (2024). *Women and Birth*, 37(4), 101625. Nordic Home Birth Cohort.
- **Type:** prospective cohort (low-risk planned home births)
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.wombi.2024.101625 · PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38754250/
- **Why it matters for LMC practice:** Among low-risk physiological home births, adjusted analyses did not show significant differences in intact perineum for water vs land birth; sphincter injury rates were low in both groups — supports nuanced counselling rather than absolute claims.
- **Confidence / caveats:** Observational; groups differed at baseline; older cohort years (2008–2013) analysed in a 2024 paper.

### 7. Midwife-led birthing centre in a humanitarian setting (Rohingya camp, Bangladesh)

- **Authors / year:** Halim, A., Abdullah, A. S. M., Rahman, F., Bazirete, O., Turkmani, S., Hughes, K., Lopes, S. C., Nove, A., Forrester, M., Scarf, V., Callander, E., & Homer, C. S. E. (2024). *PLOS Global Public Health*.
- **Type:** mixed-methods case study
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgph.0004033 · PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39656692/
- **Why it matters for LMC practice:** Women valued respectful, skilled midwifery care; challenges included language, night transport/security, and referral logistics — relevant soft lessons for **refugee/migrant and culturally diverse** maternity care in high-resource settings too.
- **Confidence / caveats:** Single-site humanitarian context; not directly generalisable to Nelson, but useful for cultural-safety and access thinking.

## Related fields

### Psychoeducational postpartum care intervention (RCT, 2025)

- **Source:** Ozhuner, Y., & Ozerdogan, N. (2025 Nov 26). *BMC Nursing*. PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41299439/
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1186/s12912-025-04079-5
- **Why it matters:** Midwife/nurse-delivered structured psychoeducation (Watson’s Human Caring Model) in Türkiye reduced PPD risk indicators and improved breastfeeding self-efficacy in an RCT — another signal that intentional postnatal education packages can matter.
- **Confidence / caveats:** Single-country FHC setting; model and staffing differ from NZ LMC practice.
